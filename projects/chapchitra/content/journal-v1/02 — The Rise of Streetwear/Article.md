# The Rise of Streetwear in Bangladesh

*A Generation Finding Its Voice Through Fashion*

From university campuses and city streets to independent fashion labels, discover how Bangladeshi streetwear brings together global influences, local culture and creative individuality.

[IMAGE:J02-01]

The tangled electrical wires above Dhaka's streets are hardly an obvious fashion reference. Yet they became the starting point for a camouflage-like pattern developed by local streetwear label Dacca. In a city where the ordinary can be visually extraordinary, even infrastructure can become design material.

Streetwear connects casual clothing with music, skateboarding, illustration and urban communities. In Bangladesh, its development can be seen through individual labels, campus culture and creative groups adapting international influences to local surroundings.

## A pattern borrowed from the city

Founded in 2019 by Jobayed Ayon, Dacca was profiled by [The Business Standard in 2023](https://www.tbsnews.net/features/mode/dacca-retooling-our-heritage-younger-generation-571946). The feature describes its use of traditional craftsmanship and the urban references behind its wire-inspired trousers.

The example is revealing because the visual language is local rather than borrowed wholesale from another fashion market. A recognisable city detail becomes a graphic element through selection and abstraction.

[IMAGE:J02-03]

## Music, movement and the graphic tee

American hip-hop, skateboarding and surfing communities helped shape modern streetwear. Sneakers, loose trousers, hoodies and graphic T-shirts travelled across communities alongside music, visual art and changing ideas about casual dress. The [Museum at the Fashion Institute of Technology's 2023 hip-hop fashion exhibition](https://www.fitnyc.edu/museum/exhibitions/hip-hop-style.php) places clothing within this wider cultural history.

The important connection is not a particular brand or a required garment. It is the way creative communities develop recognisable visual languages of their own.

## Between the campus and the screen

A student in Dhaka can encounter fashion references from Seoul, Tokyo or London online and combine them with a familiar literary or cultural reference. This mixing of influences is visible in a [2022 BIGD exploratory case study](https://bigd.bracu.ac.bd/custom-designed-fashion-a-form-of-self-expression-among-dhakas-zoomers/) among University of Dhaka students, which examined their interest in customised fashion.

Street culture also exists beyond clothing. [The Daily Star's reporting on skateboarding in Bangladesh](https://www.thedailystar.net/shout/cover-story/news/youth-wheels-1762264) provides an example of young people organising around a shared activity. Music, skating, illustration and photography can provide the settings in which clothing acquires meaning.

These examples describe particular communities. They do not tell us how large the national streetwear market is or how quickly it is growing.

## Clothes for Dhaka, not for somewhere else

The familiar international image of streetwear sometimes involves several heavy layers. In Bangladesh, a lightweight graphic T-shirt and relaxed trousers may be much more suitable for an ordinary day. A loose silhouette can be achieved without a thick hoodie or jacket.

[Our illustrated oversized styling guide](/journal/how-to-style-oversized-drop-shoulder-t-shirts/) takes a closer look at those proportions. For a longer view of local visual influences, [Bangladeshi folk art in fashion](/journal/bangladeshi-folk-art-contemporary-fashion/) explores rickshaw painting, embroidery and alpana.

[IMAGE:J02-04]

An independent illustrator or designer does not need to reproduce a famous logo to participate in streetwear. A local observation, a piece of lettering or a well-developed drawing can be the foundation of a design. [Beyond the Graphic](/journal/why-original-t-shirt-designs-matter/) explores how that creative process works.

The streets already supply the material: paint, signs, architecture, everyday objects and the people who give them meaning. What a designer does with those references is where a local fashion language begins to take shape.
