### V1 creative direction (takes precedence over original brief)

The three silhouettes are educational examples, not three mutually exclusive garment categories. Labels must identify the lowered seam, and geometry must be manually checked.

### Source creative brief (adapt to V1 override)

Create three side-by-side illustrated garment studies showing the front view of a T-shirt on the same neutral body outline.
The three examples should demonstrate:
Regular fit:
- Shoulder seam positioned near the natural shoulder.
- Comparatively closer-fitting body.
- Conventional sleeve proportions.
Oversized fit:
- Additional width through the chest and torso.
- Relaxed overall silhouette.
- Increased ease through the garment.
Oversized drop-shoulder fit:
- Clearly lowered shoulder seams.
- Relaxed body width.
- Extended upper-arm coverage created by the shoulder construction. Use subtle dotted guides and fine accent lines to indicate the natural shoulder position, garment width and bottom hem. The difference between the silhouettes must be easy to understand at a glance. The illustration must not incorrectly suggest that oversized fit and drop-shoulder construction are mutually exclusive.

Use confident ink outlines, warm cream paper, restrained Press Green and Terracotta accents and subtle printed textures. Maintain a clean instructional composition with generous negative space. Add the garment labels during final design production rather than relying on generated lettering.
