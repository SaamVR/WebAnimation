# Creative Motion Lab — Director/Cinematographer/Animator Production Bible

**Edition:** 2026-10-11 · **Status:** Commission-oriented production treatments, not claims of finished film.
**Production principle:** The product is the lead actor. Camera, physical movement, sound, graphic decoration and web interaction must all communicate a measurable concept, service, or useful customer action.

## Review hierarchy

1. **Creative Director:** In one sentence, state customer problem, hero subject, transformation, emotion and commercial payoff. Reject motion where the audience cannot name what was sold or explained. Require a 3-second product-recognition still and a final held hero frame.
2. **Cinematographer:** Produce a reference slate for lighting motivation, camera focal length (not necessarily physically exact), lens distance, silhouette, depth, texture and continuity. Every camera move earns its screen time by revealing information. Require at least three selected stills with silhouette/readability inspection.
3. **Animation Director:** Approve contact and timing, acting intention, anticipation, meaningful rest, overlapping motion, follow-through, mass and clipping. No object teleport, no unconnected pick-up and no perpetual arbitrary rotations. Review at actual playback rate and frame-by-frame at each impact.
4. **Product Experience Reviewer:** Verify browser action with real content/state, not simulated fake data presented as a real payment. Every clickable UI communicates a state and results in meaningful output. Check keyboard, reduced motion, device compatibility, fallback.
5. **Integrator:** If film, asset or browser proof does not exist on the *deployed route*, classify it as PENDING even if another export exists locally. HTTP 200, a storyboard and a rotating SVG do not count as a finished cinematic film.

## Shot grammar for every product
**Establish → Discover → Transform/Prove → Hero → Act.** Every shot specifies subject, lens/framing, camera path, primary physical action, secondary action, sound cue, transition and expected viewer takeaway. The hero hold is mandatory; interactive controls appear only after the customer understands the product.

## Nine distinct treatments

### 01 Order Journey — From Click to Doorstep
**Buyer:** retailers, fulfillment software, e-commerce studios. **Use case:** checkout confirmation animation, order-progress widget, shareable social loop.
**Narrative:** a confirmed purchase becomes a reassuring delivery. **Look:** tactile crafted cardboard, cheerful geometry; daylight, restrained paper details.
**Acting beats:** checkout ticket arrives; walls fold on explicit bottom-edge pivots; product lands with visible weight; flaps fold and tape/label seal; drone mechanically contacts parcel and flies it to an actual doorstep; status changes only on delivery.
**Camera:** clean isometric for assembly; side tracking as the package travels; locked accessible notification ending. Avoid flip/flop screen directions between shots.
**Audio:** muted by default online, optional cardboard ticks, soft impact, tape rip, subtle rotor and completion.
**Acceptance:** hinge motion does not intersect; cable/drone attachment is continuous; the on-screen state progresses and an actual Track Order demo action is shown.
**Web handoff:** animation becomes success card with order ID, timeline and tracking affordance (fictional data marked).

### 02 Living Ink — A Brand Comes Alive
**Buyer:** boutique brand identity, food/beverage packaging, creative studios. **Use case:** responsive brand animation, launch microsite, packaging story.
**Narrative:** a single ink mark grows into the fictional Verdant tea identity and packaging. **Look:** handmade pressure-sensitive brush, watercolor capillaries, generous editorial whitespace.
**Acting beats:** paper texture breathes; ink lands; organic leaf composition grows under controlled stroke width; brand lettering resolves into legible negative space; illustrated identity wraps a tea pack, website and social card.
**Camera:** mostly fixed flat surface, one justified pull-back from macro paper to completed layout. Do not force photo-real material conversion on art that is supposed to be hand-drawn.
**Audio:** paper touch, wet brush, delicate tonal finish.
**Acceptance:** legible original brand mark and packaging; deterministic brush timeline; wind/user movement that responds without breaking composition.
**Web handoff:** small animation system rather than a generic loader; hover, intro, packaging presentation.

### 03 Bloom to Bottle — From Essence to Ritual
**Buyer:** cosmetics, skincare and beauty agencies. **Use case:** product film, ingredient section, interactive beauty commerce.
**Narrative:** botanical ingredient becomes finished serum. **Look:** luminous amber glass, creamy rose backdrop, restrained macro lighting.
**Acting beats:** flower and droplets; pigment forms an essence; liquid transition resolves into physically modeled bottle; metal pipette/label settles naturally; product and botanicals occupy clean hero frame with shade/formula selection.
**Camera:** 85–100mm-style macro, motivated focal plane; finish on clean product-photography 60mm equivalent with fully legible label.
**Audio:** subtle liquid notes and glass contact, never cartoon pops.
**Acceptance:** believable glass and correct label wrapping; bottle in frame throughout reveal; working formula interaction; no fabricated ingredient claims.
**Web handoff:** ingredient storytelling followed by a SKU/variant demo, clearly a concept checkout.

### 04 AER — The Invisible Journey
**Buyer:** consumer hardware and smart-home brands. **Use case:** physically explanatory appliance hero and feature cutaway.
**Narrative:** a room's invisible air problem becomes legible and a device's role is understood. **Look:** architectural white/sage, ambient sunlight, calm air particles.
**Acting beats:** sunbeam and suspended dust; ring wakes; intake streamlines guide camera into actual filter cavity; filter layers resolve one by one; atmosphere changes symbolically; finished purifier hero with finish selector.
**Camera:** eye-level architectural start → controlled track into filter with section cut → retrace to hero; resist excessive exploded floating.
**Audio:** soft button activation, tiny fan whir and gentle air; muted by default.
**Acceptance:** filter layers plausibly correspond to model architecture; avoid unsupported filtration rates; finish actually updates mesh color/material.
**Web handoff:** clickable filter layers and real feature labels when manufacturer specifications exist.

### 05 Atelier — Made for Your Space
**Buyer:** furniture brands and custom interiors. **Use case:** configurable furniture showcase and quote flow.
**Narrative:** a personal choice becomes a tailored piece in a real room. **Look:** soft architectural warm light, tactile solid wood, fabric seams.
**Acting beats:** precise drawing; timber joinery and legs follow real assembly paths; upholstered seat settles with compression; room emerges; swatch choice changes actual chair material and finishes.
**Camera:** orthographic-ish 45-degree silhouette at beginning; macro joinery seam; eye-level architecture as room context; no ultra-wide distortion.
**Audio:** wood contact, textile rustle, subtle room ambience.
**Acceptance:** credible construction and no clipping; upholstery seam follows geometry; room lighting and shadows update correctly for materials.
**Web handoff:** choose wood/fabric, inspect dimensions and request configuration quote.

### 06 Relay — One Message, Everything Handled
**Buyer:** B2B SaaS, AI booking systems, agencies. **Use case:** functionality-driven landing page and product tour.
**Narrative:** an actionable customer enquiry moves from overload to a confirmed booking. **Look:** refined, high-readability operational UI, gentle dimensional layers.
**Acting beats:** overlapping unread enquiries; one becomes selected; details map from message to structured fields; real availability UI yields a slot; approval creates scheduled entry; confirmation moves to customer's view and inbox queue shrinks.
**Camera:** UI dolly/framing rather than arbitrary 3D spin; keep readable typography and clear hierarchy.
**Audio:** understated confirmation and handoff cues; accessible muted reading.
**Acceptance:** actual state machine, editable fictional enquiry, understandable transitions, no invented billing transactions or fake real appointment integrations.
**Web handoff:** interactive sample workflow allowing the visitor to complete one operation.

### 07 Solecraft — The Drop
**Buyer:** sneaker and sportswear brands. **Use case:** footwear launch film, short ad, accessible colorway viewer.
**Narrative:** anticipation → tactile craftsmanship → energetic physical landing → held product hero. **Look:** dark hardwood sports court, crimson rim light, realistic textured Jordan-styled shoe from user-provided FBX.
**Acting beats:** heel catches a light; leather/stitch/laces macro; three-quarter product reveal; restrained drop with heel contact and believable floor shadow; chalk accents react to impact, then clear; full product remains visible with interactive color choice.
**Camera:** 85mm-like macro texture; low 35mm landing; slow 70mm hero orbit max 35 degrees. Avoid wireframe and arbitrary full rotations.
**Audio:** distant basketball contact, fabric details, shoe landing, restrained music resolution.
**Acceptance:** genuine uploaded model visible at top of /product7; no false claim of mounted film while not hosted; no physics claims until shoes/sole/laces are independently rigged; real material variants and WebGL screenshot needed for production.
**Web handoff:** clear real model/configurator after hero hold; current staged poster is *not* final 3D/film integration.

### 08 Studio Brew — The First Cup
**Buyer:** espresso-machine and coffee equipment brands. **Use case:** equipment campaign, recipe-selector product page.
**Narrative:** a bean becomes a precise espresso ritual and desirable finished cup. **Look:** low-key brushed steel, amber crema, controlled steam; warm highlights.
**Acting beats:** bean macro; beans enter grinder; portafilter fills and locks into actual group head; extraction streams fill cup; crema settles; cup and machine become final hero with beverage choices.
**Camera:** close-up contact coverage, carefully tracked fluid flow, restrained pull-back into medium product lens.
**Audio:** grinder burr, portafilter twist, coffee extraction and cup contact.
**Acceptance:** portafilter uses realistic grip/socket mechanics; cup stays anchored; crema/steam feels materially plausible and not random particles; settings change actual UI and simulated output.
**Web handoff:** recipe-driven machine UI, no claim of connected appliance.

### 09 Kinetic Time — Every Second Matters
**Buyer:** watches/luxury accessories. **Use case:** craftsmanship film, interactive finish/strap.
**Narrative:** hidden mechanism gains meaning through detail, followed by a calm luxury product hero. **Look:** polished metal on graphite set with narrow specular highlights.
**Acting beats:** single moving tooth in darkness; mechanically motivated gear engagement; case, face and crystal align; hands travel smoothly to brand hero time; finished watch gets a narrow specular sweep and selectable strap.
**Camera:** true macro with shallow focused isolations; modest pulled-back end shot, no arbitrary 360-degree camera orbit.
**Audio:** dry ticking and minimal resolution.
**Acceptance:** mechanical causation based on a plausible watch rig or label imagery as illustrative; glass not opaque; hands separately rigged; visible materials.
**Web handoff:** inspect dial, movement and change strap/finish.

## Acceptance protocol (all nine)

**Gate 0 — Creative brief:** audience, buyer value, single narrative sentence, chosen visual reference, clearly scoped deliverables.  
**Gate 1 — Animatic:** six to eight real composition frames with shot timing and cause-effect continuity; owner reviews before render polish.  
**Gate 2 — Product asset:** held full hero still and at least one close-up; product geometry/materials actually credible when paused.  
**Gate 3 — Choreography:** anticipation, weight, overlap, grounded contact, plausible transitions, sound plan; test at 100% speed and review cut points frame-by-frame.  
**Gate 4 — Interactive web:** product is visible first, useful input changes actual subject, fallback, reduced motion, mobile, keyboard and no fake integration.  
**Gate 5 — Film deliverable:** verified 16:9 H.264 or WebM export, real frames, playback duration, source provenance and readable hold.  
**Gate 6 — Public deployment:** published /productN/ contains the *actual* accepted film or motion, media URLs load, controls work, deep links and homepage are correct. A text-only storyboard is not a PASS.
