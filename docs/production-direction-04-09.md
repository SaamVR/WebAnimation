# Product Motion Collection — Direction Book / October 10, 2026

## Intent
Sell services, not abstractions. The viewer must understand: **what product is this, why does it matter, what interaction could a brand commission?** Motion should depict causation, not disconnected effects. The six new ideas complement the three existing prototypes and share one accessible animation presentation system.

## Common production rules
- Establish one hero product and narrative hierarchy before adding polish.
- Block the story first; every shot must have an identifiable focal object, physical connection, preparation, action, settle, and payoff.
- Stage readable products against complementary value structures (light ceramic against mineral backdrops, bronze against a deep coffee palette).
- Use asymmetrical anticipation and overlap rather than a single global tween.
- Supply user-operable playback, timeline scrubbing, product choices, and a presentation still.
- Keep concept claims verifiable. No invented technical specifications, scientific claims, real checkouts, or live customer data.
- Export a deterministic video from the same temporal function used in the browser.

## Project 04: AER — The invisible, made visible (20.5 s)
**Buyer**: smart-home appliances and consumer hardware.
**Cinematic treatment**: sunlit architectural room, sage/stone materials, gently suspended particulate matter, highlighted airflow.
**Beats**: 0–20% ambient reveal; 18–33% fan activation with incoming streamlines; 33–49% housing disassembles, filter becomes visible; 49–81% layered cutaway inspection and reassembly; 71–100% renewed atmosphere and optimized-status product payoff.
**UI**: Mist, Sand, Graphite.
**Commercial use**: hero film plus product-layer inspection. **Risk**: airflow is a visual metaphor, not a measured CAD/CFD model.

## Project 05: ATELIER — A sketch becomes a space (22 s)
**Buyer**: furniture makers and bespoke interiors.
**Cinematic treatment**: warm wall, geometric sunlight, timber grain and upholstered forms; quiet editorial serif/graphic counterpoint.
**Beats**: 0–31% engineering-pencil guidework; 22–43% wooden frame, legs and arms settle; 41–67% soft upholstery descends with slight overlap; 69–84% room appears; 80–100% fabric/wood finish reveal.
**UI**: Linen & Oak, Sage & Walnut, Terracotta & Smoked.
**Commercial use**: configured product exploration. **Risk**: geometry/stitching illustrative rather than precision manufactured CAD.

## Project 06: RELAY — From enquiry to done (18 s)
**Buyer**: service SaaS, CRM vendors, booking/automation consultants.
**Cinematic treatment**: dark control-room UI with pale form panels, mint signal traces and clearly staged data transfer.
**Beats**: 0–18% incoming enquiry; 23–40% entity extraction; 45–64% schedule lock; 67–83% payment link; 84–100% booking confirmation.
**UI**: Booking, Consultation, Service Request (scenario labels; the depicted prototype is a single demonstration workflow).
**Commercial use**: feature landing pages and SaaS explainers. **Risk**: fictional schedule and payment; no real integrations.

## Project 07: SOLECRAFT — Engineered to move (20.4 s)
**Buyer**: footwear and apparel e-commerce.
**Cinematic treatment**: gallery-white cyclorama, knitted surfaces, restraint in stitching, accent midsole.
**Beats**: 0–19% quiet product anticipation; 19–50% upper material formation; 36–57% cushioning base arrives; 56–78% laces finish; 76–100% material callout.
**UI**: Sandstone, Skyline, Fieldstone.
**Commercial use**: launch animation and SKU choice. **Risk**: illustrative, not real-world footwear physics or lab performance.

## Project 08: STUDIO BREW — The art of extraction (19 s)
**Buyer**: espresso machine makers, coffee gear brands.
**Cinematic treatment**: dark studio, brushed metal, copper/coffee notes, aromatic steam.
**Beats**: 0–20% floating beans; 20–40% grinding disappears into machine; 43–68% pour begins; 53–87% steam rises; 79–100% ready cup and recipe callout.
**UI**: Espresso, Oat Latte, Decaf.
**Commercial use**: product film and recipe-driven explainer. **Risk**: liquid and brewing process are graphic simulations.

## Project 09: KINETIC TIME — Precision is poetry (21 s)
**Buyer**: watch brands and luxury accessories.
**Cinematic treatment**: charcoal stage, controlled gold/chrome highlights, exposed movement.
**Beats**: 0–43% strap and components appear; 43–76% case resolves; 75–94% gears and hands animate; final callout.
**UI**: Champagne, Stainless, Warm Gold.
**Commercial use**: premium detail section and finishing selection. **Risk**: gear train illustrates craftsmanship, not a physically validated movement.

## What is complete in this iteration
Shared browser shell; six scene render functions, six directly accessible project pages, three variants per new page, timeline player/scrubbing, data-copy interaction, nine cards and filtering on a responsive gallery, legacy projects 1–3 unchanged, export support added for all new scene durations, and static-site deployment configuration retained.

## Next fidelity pass
These current scenes are **stylized motion prototypes**, not yet high-end 3D cinematic films. Before representing them as premium client-ready production, prioritize (1) art-led shotboard and animatic reviews, (2) stronger lighting/material fidelity, (3) physical secondary action and more refined contact timing, (4) sound design, (5) device/browser GPU performance profiling, (6) polished MP4 reels and client case studies. This roadmap is a quality distinction, not an assertion that those features are done.
